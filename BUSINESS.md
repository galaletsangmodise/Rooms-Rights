# Rooms&Rights — Business Plan

**Rent fairly. Live securely. Know your rights.**


## 1. The problem

Across South African townships, thousands of households rent out backrooms and backyard rooms, and thousands of tenants live in them. Almost all of it is informal: a handshake, cash rent, no receipt, no written terms.

**Who has it:** backyard tenants and the small landlords who rent to them.

**How often:** every month, at every rent date, and every time something breaks, rent is late, or someone leaves.

**What it costs today:**
- Tenants lose deposits, have no proof of payment, and can be locked out or have electricity cut with no record of what was agreed.
- Landlords, who are usually ordinary households relying on the income, lose rent, argue over damage, and can break the law by accident (for example by locking out a tenant or cutting services).
- Disputes escalate because nothing was written down.

**Why existing help falls short:**
- The Rental Housing Tribunal is free and does not need a lawyer, but the Gauteng tribunal has only four inspectors serving the whole province and aims to finish matters within about three months. It also cannot hear eviction applications.
- The tribunal received 585 complaints between April and June 2026, so demand for help is already visible.
- Lawyers are unaffordable for a R1,200-a-month room.



---

## 2. The customer

**Who uses it**
- **Landlord (the payer):** a household renting out one to five backrooms.
- **Tenant (free user):** a person renting one of those rooms.
- **Paralegal / advice office (supporting role):** reviews templates and, in future, escalated cases.

**Who pays:** the landlord. The landlord gets the most value (protected rental income, records, legal safety), has a regular income, and is the person who decides to start using the tool. Tenants join free through the landlord's invite.

**First customer profile:** a landlord in my own area with two or more rooms, a smartphone, and WhatsApp.

---

## 3. The solution (one sentence)

Rooms&Rights is a mobile-first web app that helps backyard landlords and tenants put their agreement in writing, keep every document, payment and receipt in one place, and resolve disputes before they become evictions.

**What it does on day one**
1. Landlord and tenant log in with their own roles.
2. Landlord sets up a property and rooms (no public listings, because people find rooms door to door).
3. Landlord creates a written agreement or records oral terms, prints it or shares it, and invites the tenant with a link, code or QR.
4. Both sides keep documents in an Evidence Vault.
5. Landlord records rent and deposit payments, and both sides see receipts and balances.

**Next:** a Dispute Centre and a Rental Housing Tribunal complaint pack generated from the records the app already holds.

---

## 4. The impact

**What changes if we succeed**
- Agreements move from "we agreed verbally" to a written, shared record.
- Every payment has a receipt, so "I paid" can be proved.
- Landlords learn what they must not do (no lock-outs, no cutting services) before they make a costly mistake.
- Disputes are settled earlier, and tribunal complaints arrive organised.

**How we measure it**

| Impact | Metric | Pilot target [VALIDATE] |
|---|---|---|
| Agreements in writing | Rooms with a signed or recorded agreement | 50 rooms |
| Payment proof | Share of rent payments logged with a receipt | 80% |
| Fewer disputes | Disputes resolved without escalation | Track baseline, then improve |
| Adoption | Tenants who accept the invite within 7 days | 70% |
| Legal safety | Landlords who view the "know your rights" guidance | Track |

## 5. The money

**Model:** monthly subscription per room, paid by the landlord. Tenants are free.

**Price hypothesis:** between R30 and R60 per room per month, low enough to feel like a small share of a R1,200 rent, with the first month free. Test this in landlord interviews before fixing it.

**Illustration (not a forecast):** 100 landlords with 2 rooms each at R50 per room = 200 rooms × R50 = **R10,000 a month**.

**Possible later revenue**
- Paid add-ons: notice generator, extra storage, premium reports
- Sponsorship by municipalities, NGOs and legal clinics who want better data and fewer disputes
- Referral fees from partner attorneys for matters outside our scope

**Costs to track:** hosting and database, SMS/WhatsApp messages, payment fees, attorney review of templates, support time. At this stage, hosting is low-cost.

**Why the tenant joins:** the landlord asks them to, the tenant gets a free copy of the agreement and proof of every payment.

## 7. The competition

| Alternative | What it offers | Gap |
|---|---|---|
| Nothing (handshake, WhatsApp, notebook) | Free, familiar | No proof, no structure, disputes escalate |
| Rental Housing Tribunal | Free dispute resolution | Slow, stretched, cannot handle evictions, not a record-keeping tool |
| Legal Aid and community advice offices | Free advice | Capacity limited, no ongoing paperwork or records |
| Online legal document platforms (e.g. LegitLaw, Legal Legends, Easy Contracts) | Affordable contracts | Mostly aimed at SMEs and startups, not backyard rooms or low-data users |
| Rental and property management software | Tools for property managers | Built for agents and larger portfolios, priced and designed for a different customer |

**Why Rooms&Rights is better for this customer**
- Built for backyard rooms, not generic contracts or property managers
- Supports oral agreements (recorded terms), because that is how most rooms are rented
- Works on a basic phone, low data, and can print a paper copy
- Records stay useful later: agreement, receipts and documents become the evidence pack for a dispute
- Neutral between landlord and tenant, so both sides can trust the record
- Local language and local context

## 9. Legal position and risks

**Positioning:** Rooms&Rights provides legal *information* and paperwork tools. It is not a law firm and does not give legal advice. South African law reserves certain legal services for admitted attorneys and advocates, so the product avoids calling itself an "AI lawyer" and avoids giving case-specific legal advice.

**Safeguards**
- Agreement templates and rights content are reviewed by a practising attorney before launch.
- Clear "information, not legal advice" notices.
- Strong warnings that lock-outs and cutting services are unlawful.
- Evictions go through the courts, and the app refers users to paralegals or attorneys.

**Risks**

| Risk | Plan |
|---|---|
| Regulatory (unauthorised legal practice) | Information-only positioning, attorney review, human escalation |
| Low trust or adoption | Pilot with a known community, printed QR agreements, Setswana support |
| Landlord-paid tool seen as one-sided | Neutral content, tenant has full access to their own records |
| Data protection (POPIA) | Collect minimal data, restrict access, ID copies optional with consent |
| Low connectivity | Lightweight pages, image compression, printable fallback |
| Willingness to pay | Test price in interviews, free first month, adjust per room price |
